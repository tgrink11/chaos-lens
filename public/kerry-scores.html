What this page does

Every weekday after the market closes, the 1880 Quant Analyzer reviews every stock on Kerry's Tell Sheet, Watchlist, and BUS! Research lists. For each one, it asks two independent questions, then combines them into a single Rating that tells you whether the stock is accumulating, distributing, or showing no clear pattern.

The Rating column is the one column you need to read. Every other column is an input that fed into the Rating — they're there for transparency, not as competing recommendations.

The two questions, and how they combine

Question 1: Where does the price sit relative to its moving averages? (the "SMA tier")

  • SPECULATIVE — price is below the 200-day SMA. Nothing structurally good happens below the line of last resort.
  • WATCH — price has reclaimed the 200-day but not yet the 62-day. Early-stage recovery.
  • TRENDING — price is above both the 62-day and 200-day SMAs. The medium-term trend is up.
  • STRONG — price is above all three (15 / 62 / 200), with the SMAs themselves stacked bullishly (15 > 62 > 200). Best structural setup.

Question 2: Does the price action confirm a real trend? (the "fractal confirmation")

A stock can be above its moving averages and still be wandering randomly. Three fractal measurements check whether the trend is real:

  • Hurst ≥ 0.55 — price persists in one direction (not a random walk).
  • Box dim ≤ 1.20 — price follows a smooth path (not chaotic).
  • Lambda ≤ 1.25 — volatility is uniform (not gappy / clustered).

All three pass → STRONG confirmation. One or two pass → INDECISIVE. Zero pass → WEAK (random or chaotic price action).

Step by step: how to use the page each morning

  1. Open the page. The Rating column tells you the bottom line. Hover any Rating badge for a one-sentence plain-English explanation.
  2. Sort is already Rating descending — STRONG ACCUMULATION at the top, DISTRIBUTION / SPECULATIVE at the bottom. Your accumulation candidates are at the top of the list.
  3. The Fractals: Trend Confirmation columns (yellow tint) show the inputs that drove the fractal confirmation half of the Rating — the directional reads, H / D / Λ, Conviction, and Mood.
  4. The Trend columns (blue tint) show the 15 / 62 / 200-day SMAs with green ▲ if price is above and red ▼ if below. That's how you see at a glance which tier a stock is in.
  5. For any stock you want to dig deeper on, click Get AI take. Claude follows the same 4-step reasoning the page uses (SMA tier → fractal confirmation → combine → action implication), and closes with a required "10th Man" section arguing the contrarian case.
  6. Filter pills narrow to Tell Sheet / Watchlist / BUS! Research. Search box jumps to a specific ticker. "Analyze any ticker" runs the same analysis on any 1–5 letter US symbol.

The Trading Range column

Separate from the Rating, the Trading Range column shows the probable 15-day price band (LRR – TRR) given current volatility. The marker shows where today's close sits within the band: bottom = favorable entry, middle = no edge, top = stretched.

The Rating tells you whether to be in the stock; the Trading Range tells you when within the next 15 days the price is at a good entry. Use them together: an ACCUMULATING rating at the bottom of its Trading Range is the highest-quality long signal you can find.

⚡ The Intraday overlay button

The Rating column reflects the most recent end-of-day close — that's what the engine uses, and what the model is designed for. During the trading day, you can click the ⚡ Intraday overlay button to pull live prices and see whether today's intraday action has moved any stock past its band boundaries.

Important: the fractals, the SMAs, and the band boundaries themselves do not change intraday — those are EOD-only quantities. What changes is where today's live price sits inside yesterday's band. If a stock gapped up past TRR (top of band), the Rating can flip from HOLD to TRIM, or TRIM to SELL. If it dropped to LRR (bottom of band), it can flip toward BUY. When the intraday read differs from the EOD read, the row shows both side by side with a purple arrow between them.

This is a discretionary overlay, not a replacement for the EOD read. The model's edge comes from filtering out intraday noise. Use the overlay when you're actively watching a name during the session — not as a permanent state.
